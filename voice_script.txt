[0:10-0:22]  WHAT IT IS
ON SCREEN: title card / repo name, or freeze on the neon dashboard.

  "This is sentinel-imx-sys: a tiny anomaly-detection daemon for the
   NXP i.MX 8M Plus. It learns what normal logs look like for your device,
   and alerts the moment something looks wrong."


[0:22-0:58]  ARCHITECTURE + HARDWARE
ON SCREEN: the "System architecture" diagram from the README.

  "Here's the picture. The hardware is an i.MX 8M Plus -- a quad-core
   Arm chip with a Vivante neural-processing unit built in.
   The daemon plugs into two live sources: the systemd journal -- which
   carries both kernel messages and userspace service logs -- and the
   D-Bus system bus, all through a single event loop.
   Every log line is stripped down to a stable template -- process IDs,
   addresses and numbers masked out -- then encoded into a small
   64-value integer vector.
   That vector is scored by a TensorFlow Lite autoencoder that runs on
   the NPU through NXP's VX delegate. If the score is too high, the daemon
   runs an action script that you control -- light an LED, restart a
   service, page your fleet."


[0:58-1:42]  HOW IT WORKS
ON SCREEN: the "Training & detection workflow" diagram from the README.

  "So how does it actually decide? It's an autoencoder -- trained only on
   normal traffic, so anything unfamiliar it reconstructs badly. High
   error means anomaly.
   Three steps. First, collect: the daemon records normal log templates
   on the board. Second, train: on your host, in a Docker container, we
   re-encode those templates with the exact same encoder the daemon uses,
   train the network, quantize it to INT8, and auto-calibrate the alert
   threshold. Third, detect: for every new line, reconstruct, measure the
   error, and compare to that threshold.
   The trick that makes it honest is normalizing each event -- so the
   model scores the pattern of a line, not just its length."


[1:42-2:00]  CLOSE + CALL TO ACTION
ON SCREEN: press 'n' -> NPU load meter climbs; then repo link / license.

  "And it's basically free: the CPU barely moves, because the NPU does
   the inference -- here it is, fully loaded.
   Real-time, on-device anomaly detection for embedded Linux.
   It's open source, Apache-2.0 -- link in the description. Go build."
